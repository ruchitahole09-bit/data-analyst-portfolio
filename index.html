Business Problem

A Brazilian e-commerce marketplace needed to understand whether its growth was being driven by genuine repeat demand or one-time transactions — and which products and regions deserved inventory and marketing priority.

Data Source

The Olist Brazilian e-commerce public dataset: 9 relational tables spanning orders, customers, products, sellers, reviews, and payments — over 100,000 orders.

Methodology

Cleaned and joined all 9 tables in Python/Pandas, then modeled the data into a Snowflake star schema (4 dimension tables + 1 fact table) to support fast, analysis-ready querying at scale.

Tools & Technologies

Python, Pandas, Snowflake (SQL), Power BI, regression-based time series forecasting, GitHub for version control.

Analysis Performed

  • 25+ advanced SQL queries — window functions, CTEs, self-joins, and RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) scoring — run against the Snowflake warehouse
  • RFM-based customer segmentation across 93,000+ customers to separate loyal buyers from one-time purchasers
  • ABC analysis on product categories to identify revenue concentration
  • A 3-month revenue forecast using regression-based time series decomposition, validated against held-out historical data

Key Findings

  • 97% of customers had purchased exactly once — repeat-purchase behavior was the exception, not the norm
  • The top 20% of product categories generated over 80% of total revenue (R$13.2M), a textbook Pareto pattern hiding in plain sight
  • A corrupted timestamp field was silently breaking date-based joins during warehouse loading — caught and fixed before it reached the dashboard

Business Recommendations

  • Redirect retention budget toward the post-purchase window, where the data showed the steepest drop-off
  • Prioritize inventory and ad spend on the top-quintile categories identified by ABC analysis
  • Use the 3-month forecast to right-size seasonal stock ahead of demand swings, not after them

Business Problem

Lenders routinely rely on credit score as the headline risk signal — but a portfolio's actual default pattern doesn't always agree. The goal: build a risk model that's tested against the data rather than assumed from convention, and connect it to revenue so risk decisions account for what a customer is actually worth.

Data Source

A 255,347-record consumer loan dataset (Kaggle) with demographic, credit, and loan-term fields. Realistic data-quality issues — missing values, duplicates, inconsistent casing — were deliberately reintroduced under a fixed random seed to authentically demonstrate the cleaning methodology, and disclosed transparently in the project documentation.

Methodology

Cleaned and validated the dataset in Python, ran exploratory analysis to test which variables actually predicted default, then built a multi-factor Risk Score whose weights were set by the correlation evidence — not by textbook assumption.

Tools & Technologies

Python (Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn), MySQL, Power BI with DAX, Claude API used as a development accelerator for debugging and workflow design.

Analysis Performed

  • Missing-value pattern analysis (MCAR testing) before choosing an imputation strategy, rather than imputing by default
  • Correlation analysis across all numeric risk factors against default outcome to weight the Risk Score evidence-first
  • 30+ SQL queries in MySQL — CTEs, RANK, DENSE_RANK, PERCENT_RANK, and running totals — to quantify risk concentration and revenue contribution by segment
  • Feature engineering: a composite Risk Score, Revenue Score, quartile-based Customer Value Tiers, and a Financial Health Index

Key Findings

  • Credit Score alone showed almost no correlation with default (-0.03) — a result that ran against the conventional underwriting assumption
  • Age and Interest Rate were the strongest individual predictors, which directly reshaped how the Risk Score was weighted
  • The resulting composite score separated High-Risk accounts (22.4% actual default) from Low-Risk accounts (4.1%) — a 5.4× gap that validated the multi-factor approach
  • The top revenue tier (Platinum) generated 17× the average revenue of the bottom tier (Bronze)

Business Recommendations

  • Replace single-variable credit-score gating with the validated multi-factor Risk Score in underwriting review
  • Route the "high-risk, high-revenue" overlap segment to manual review instead of automatic approval or decline
  • Make employment-status disclosure mandatory at application — "Unknown" employment correlated with elevated default risk nearly matching the Unemployed segment
